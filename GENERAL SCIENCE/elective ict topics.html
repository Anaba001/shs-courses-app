Introduction to Elective ICT

Elective Information and Communication Technology (ICT) for SHS is designed to build students’ understanding of digital systems, software, data, and networks. The course connects theory and practice, enabling students to use technology responsibly, solve problems, and communicate information effectively.

ICT in senior high school is not just about using devices; it is also about learning how digital systems work, how information is represented, and how technology impacts society. The subject emphasizes computational thinking, design, and ethical use of technology.



Elective ICT equips learners with the practical and analytical skills required to thrive in an increasingly digital economy.

SHS ICT fundamentals

ICT Foundations

ICT Foundations introduce the key building blocks of information technology: hardware, software, communication, data, and problem solving. Each foundation area helps students understand how modern systems are designed, built, and used.

Hardware Systems

Hardware refers to the physical components of a computer system. Students learn the roles of input devices, output devices, storage, and processing units.

Input Devices: keyboards, mice, scanners, microphones and cameras that allow users to enter data and commands.

Output Devices: monitors, printers, speakers, and projectors that present information in visual, printed, or audio form.

Storage & Memory: primary memory (RAM), secondary storage (hard drives, SSDs, flash drives), and cloud storage.

Processing Units: central processing units (CPU), graphics processing units (GPU), and the concept of bus systems that move data within the machine.

Software and Operating Systems

Software is the set of instructions that tells hardware what to do. Students study both system software and application software.

System Software: operating systems, device drivers, and utilities that manage computer resources.

Application Software: word processors, spreadsheets, presentation tools, web browsers, and specialised ICT applications.

Utilities & Productivity: file managers, antivirus programs, compression tools, and software that helps maintain and organise systems.

Networks and Communication

ICT connects devices and people through networks. This topic covers the types, structures, and protocols that support communication.

Network Types: local area networks (LANs), wide area networks (WANs), and the internet.

Network Topologies: bus, star, ring, mesh, and hybrid configurations.

Internet and Email: how browsers, email, and web servers work together to share information.

Cybersecurity: basic security measures, passwords, firewalls, antivirus software, and safe online habits.

Data Management

Data Management teaches students how information is collected, organised, stored, and protected.

Database Fundamentals: tables, records, fields, primary keys, and queries.

Data Representation: text, numbers, images, audio, and binary encoding.

File Management: naming conventions, folder structures, backups, and archives.

Programming and Problem Solving

Programming teaches students how to write and test instructions that computers can execute.

Algorithm Design: solving problems with clear, step-by-step procedures.

Pseudocode & Flowcharts: visual methods for planning programs before coding.

Programming Languages: introduction to languages like Python, HTML, CSS, and simple scripting.

Web Development

Web Development shows how websites are built and published.

HTML/CSS Basics: structure, headings, paragraphs, links, images, and styling.

Website Design: usability, layout, navigation, and responsive pages.

E-Commerce and Online Services: how online shops, banking, and digital learning platforms work.

Emerging Technologies

This section explores the new technologies shaping the future of ICT.

AI, Robotics & IoT

Artificial intelligence, robotics, and the Internet of Things connect devices and automate tasks. Students learn how smart systems can sense, learn, and act.

Mobile Computing

Mobile computing covers smartphones, tablets, and apps. It explains how portable devices access networks and manage location-based services.

Cloud Computing

Cloud Computing introduces online storage and services, showing how users can access software and data from anywhere.

ICT in Society

ICT has a deep impact on daily life, education, business, and ethics.

ICT in Education

Technology supports teaching, learning, research, and digital literacy in schools. Students explore e-learning, distance education, and digital classroom tools.

ICT in Business

Businesses use ICT to manage operations, communicate, and deliver services. Topics include online marketing, digital records, and mobile commerce.

Digital Citizenship

Digital Citizenship covers responsible online behaviour, privacy, intellectual property, and ethical use of technology.

Being a good digital citizen means using ICT safely, respectfully, and responsibly in every online environment.

Career and Future Trends

Elective ICT prepares students for careers in software development, networking, cybersecurity, data science, web design, and more. The future includes continued innovation in AI, smart systems, cloud services, and digital transformation.

1. Crucial in all industries

As businesses become more tech-dependent, the need for IT professionals is growing in sectors like finance, healthcare, retail, and more.

2. High demand

The demand for IT skills ensures job stability and growth opportunities for graduates.

3.Wide range of roles

An IT degree opens doors to various specializations, including software development, cybersecurity, database management, and network administration.

4. Industry flexibility

IT professionals can work in numerous fields, including finance, media, gaming, and the public sector, offering flexibility and a variety of career paths.

5. Good earning potential

IT roles often come with competitive salaries, which increase significantly with experience and postgraduate qualifications.

6. Career progression

IT provides a clear path for career advancement, with opportunities to move into management and more specialized positions, such as cyber scurity and so on.

7. Impactful work

The skills gained in IT allow you to contribute to innovations that are shaping our daily lives, from scientific research to making daily tasks more efficient.

8. Problem-solving

IT graduates are essential for creating the programs and gadgets that solve problems and drive progress in fields like artificial intelligence and cybersecurity.

9. Hard and soft skills

An IT course develops crucial technical abilities like programming, data analysis, and cloud computing, alongside vital soft skills like problem-solving, teamwork, and communication.

10. Lifelong learning

The field requires continuous learning, which helps you stay relevant and adaptable in a fast-changing technological environment.


After obtaining a degree in IT, there are many pathways that graduates can take. Some may choose to pursue advanced studies in IT or related fields, while others may choose to gain professional certifications in order to enhance their skills and job prospects. Below are some common career paths in IT and the professional qualifications required for each:

1 Software Developer

To become a software developer, you will typically need a degree or diploma in computer science, software engineering, or a related field. You may also benefit from obtaining certifications in programming languages such as Java, Python, or C++. Some popular certifications for software developers include Microsoft Certified Solutions Developer (MCSD) and Oracle Certified Professional: Java SE 11 Developer.

2 Cybersecurity Analyst

A degree or diploma in computer science, information technology, or a related field is typically required. Certifications in Certified Information Systems Security Professional (CISSP), Certified Ethical Hacker, or CompTIA Security+ can be helpful in advancing your career in this field.

3 Network Administrator

To become a network administrator, you will typically need a degree or diploma in computer science, information technology, or a related field. You may also benefit from obtaining certifications in networking technologies such as Cisco, CompTIA Network+, or Juniper. Some popular certifications for network administrators include Cisco Certified Network Associate (CCNA) and CompTIA Network+ Certification.

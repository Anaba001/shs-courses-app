WELCOME TO FOOD AND NUTRITION

WHY DO YOU WANT TO STUDY FOOD AND NUTRITION?

Food and nutrition teaches how to choose, prepare, and consume food in a way that promotes health, growth, and well-being.

Introduction to Food and Nutrition

Food and nutrition is the study of how food supports the body and contributes to good health. It involves understanding the nutrients in food, how they function, how they are used by the body, and how meals should be planned to meet human needs. Nutrition is a basic part of daily life because food provides the energy and nutrients required for growth, repair, and proper functioning of the body.

Good nutrition helps prevent disease, improve productivity, strengthen immunity, and promote healthy living. The knowledge of food and nutrition is therefore important for individuals, families, schools, and communities. It guides proper feeding habits and helps people make better food choices.



Formula: Good Health = Balanced Diet + Safe Food + Regular Activity
Food and nutrition helps people understand how diet affects health and daily life.

Food and nutrition overview

Nutrients and Their Functions

Nutrients are substances found in food that the body needs for growth, energy, repair, and protection. The major nutrients are carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, minerals, and water. Each nutrient has a special role in maintaining health and supporting body functions. A deficiency or excess of certain nutrients can lead to poor health.

A well-planned diet must include all these nutrients in the right amounts. The balance of nutrients is more important than simply eating large quantities of food.



Nutrients are the building blocks that keep the body strong and healthy.

Nutrients note

Carbohydrates

Carbohydrates are the body’s main source of energy. They are found in foods such as rice, cassava, yam, maize, bread, potatoes, and fruits. Carbohydrates are broken down into glucose, which the body uses to fuel activities such as walking, studying, working, and playing.

Too little carbohydrate may cause low energy, while too much may contribute to weight gain. A balanced intake is therefore essential.

Formula: Energy from Carbohydrates = 4 kcal per gram
Carbohydrates provide the body with immediate energy for daily activities.

Carbohydrate note

Proteins

Proteins are nutrients needed for growth, repair, and maintenance of body tissues. They are important for building muscles, organs, enzymes, and hormones. Good sources of protein include fish, meat, eggs, beans, milk, groundnuts, and soy products.

Protein is especially important for children, pregnant women, athletes, and people recovering from illness or injury.

Formula: Energy from Protein = 4 kcal per gram
Proteins help the body grow and repair itself.

Protein note

Fats and oils

Fats and oils are concentrated sources of energy and help the body absorb vitamins A, D, E, and K. They also protect organs and help maintain body temperature. Sources include butter, palm oil, groundnut oil, avocado, nuts, and fish oil.

Although fats are important, excessive intake can increase the risk of heart disease and weight gain. Healthy fats should be eaten in moderation.

Formula: Energy from Fats = 9 kcal per gram
Fats provide concentrated energy and help the body absorb important vitamins.

Fat note

Vitamins and minerals

Vitamins and minerals are needed in small amounts but are essential for good health. Vitamins help regulate body processes, while minerals support bone formation, blood health, nerve function, and fluid balance. Examples include vitamin A, vitamin C, vitamin D, iron, calcium, and iodine.

These nutrients are found in fruits, vegetables, dairy products, eggs, fish, and whole grains. Deficiency of these nutrients may lead to disease and poor growth.

Vitamins and minerals support the body’s many vital functions.

Vitamin and mineral note

Meal Planning and Diet

Meal planning is the process of deciding what food to prepare and eat over a period of time. It helps families and individuals make better food choices, save money, reduce waste, and meet nutritional needs. Meal planning also helps manage time and improve food variety.

A good meal plan should consider age, activity level, health status, cultural preferences, and available resources. It should also include a balance of nutrients and variety of foods.



Meal planning makes healthy eating practical, organized, and affordable.

Meal planning note

Balanced diet

A balanced diet contains the right amount of food from the main food groups. It should include energy-giving foods, body-building foods, and protective foods. A balanced diet supports growth, improves immunity, and prevents nutrient deficiency.

For example, a balanced meal may include staples such as rice or yam, a protein food such as fish or beans, vegetables, fruits, and water. This provides the body with the nutrients it needs.

Formula: Balanced Diet = Staples + Proteins + Vegetables + Fruits + Water
A balanced diet provides the body with the nutrients required for good health.

Balanced diet note

Meal preparation

Meal preparation involves washing, cutting, cooking, seasoning, and serving food. Good meal preparation helps preserve nutrients and improves the appearance and taste of meals. It also makes cooking easier and prevents food waste.

Proper preparation methods such as steaming, boiling, and baking are often healthier than frying. Choosing the right cooking method can improve both nutrition and flavour.

Good meal preparation helps preserve nutrients and improve the quality of food.

Meal preparation note

Special diets

Special diets are eating patterns designed for people with particular health conditions or needs. Examples include diabetic diets, low-salt diets, vegetarian diets, and high-protein diets. These diets are designed to manage health problems and improve wellbeing.

Special diets should be planned carefully so that they remain nutritious and suitable for the individual. They may require the guidance of a nutrition professional.

Special diets meet the unique nutritional needs of certain individuals.

Special diet note

Budgeting for food

Budgeting for food means planning how money will be spent on meals and groceries. It helps families buy nutritious foods at affordable prices. Good budgeting reduces waste and makes sure that food needs are met within available resources.

A food budget may be based on family size, preferences, seasonality, and local market prices. Efficient planning can improve both nutrition and financial stability.

Formula: Food Budget = Planned Monthly Food Needs - Cost of Planned Meals
Food budgeting helps families use money wisely while meeting nutritional needs.

Food budgeting note

Food Safety and Hygiene

Food safety is the practice of handling, storing, and preparing food in ways that prevent contamination and disease. Safe food helps protect the health of everyone who eats it. Hygiene is a key part of food safety because poor practices can introduce germs and harmful substances.

Food safety includes keeping food clean, cooking it properly, avoiding cross-contamination, and storing it at the correct temperature. It is essential in homes, schools, markets, and restaurants.



Food safety protects people from illness and contamination.

Food safety note

Food storage

Food storage refers to keeping food in suitable conditions so that it remains safe and nutritious. This includes refrigeration, freezing, drying, and using sealed containers. Proper storage slows microbial growth and prevents contamination.

Food should be stored according to type, temperature, and shelf life. Labeling and rotation also help reduce waste.

Safe food storage helps preserve quality and prevent spoilage.

Food storage note

Food hygiene

Food hygiene focuses on cleanliness in the preparation and handling of food. It includes washing hands, cleaning surfaces, using clean utensils, and keeping raw and cooked foods separate. Good hygiene lowers the risk of food-borne disease.

Food hygiene is especially important when preparing food for children, the elderly, or patients.

Food hygiene prevents contamination and protects health.

Food hygiene note

Food preservation

Food preservation is the process of keeping food from spoiling for long periods. Common methods include drying, salting, freezing, canning, pickling, and fermentation. Preservation helps reduce food waste, especially in communities with limited storage facilities.

Preservation makes food available beyond its fresh season and contributes to food security.

Food preservation extends the life of food and reduces waste.

Food preservation note

Food-borne diseases

Food-borne diseases are illnesses caused by eating contaminated food. They may result from bacteria, viruses, parasites, or chemicals. Common symptoms include stomach pain, vomiting, diarrhea, and fever. Proper hygiene and safe food handling can prevent many of these illnesses.

Awareness of food safety is therefore essential for household health and public wellbeing.

Food-borne diseases can be prevented by safe food handling and hygiene.

Food-borne disease note

Nutrition for Life Stages

Nutrition needs change throughout life. Infants, children, adolescents, adults, and elders all need different amounts and types of food. Understanding these changing needs helps promote health at every stage of life.

Good nutrition at each stage supports growth, development, energy, immunity, and recovery. The right diet improves quality of life and reduces the risk of disease.



Nutrition needs change with age, growth, and life demands.

Life-stage nutrition note

Infants and children

Infants and children need foods rich in nutrients for growth and development. Breastfeeding, milk, fruits, vegetables, grains, and protein-rich foods are especially important. Children also need enough energy for learning, movement, and play.

Poor nutrition during childhood can affect physical and mental development. Therefore, feeding practices should be careful and balanced.

Children need nutritious food to support growth and learning.

Child nutrition note

Adolescents

Adolescents need more energy and nutrients because they undergo rapid growth and development. Their diet should include enough protein, iron, calcium, and vitamins. Healthy eating during adolescence helps support physical growth, academic performance, and reproductive health.

Good nutrition at this stage can also reduce the risk of future health problems.

Adolescents need balanced nutrition to support rapid growth and development.

Adolescent nutrition note

Adults

Adults need food that supports maintenance of health, energy, and productivity. A balanced diet helps prevent lifestyle diseases such as obesity, hypertension, diabetes, and heart disease. Adults should also pay attention to portion size and regular physical activity.

Nutrition habits in adulthood strongly affect long-term health.

Good nutrition in adulthood supports long-term health and productivity.

Adult nutrition note

Elders

Elders need food that supports health maintenance, digestion, and immunity. Their diet should be easy to chew and digest, while still providing enough nutrients. Adequate hydration, protein, calcium, and vitamins are particularly important.

Nutrition for older adults helps reduce frailty and improve quality of life.

Formula: Healthy Ageing = Balanced Diet + Hydration + Activity
Good nutrition helps older people stay healthy, active, and independent.

Elder nutrition note
